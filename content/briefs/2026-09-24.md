## 1. Hot areas (3–5)

### A. Training data & RL environments as the scarce commodity
**Why this week:** Capital printed a clear price on **data-as-a-service** for frontier labs. On Sep 22, **Snorkel AI** raised a **$350M Series E at a $3.5B valuation** — nearly triple the **$1.3B** mark from its Series D ~17 months prior — led by **Insight Partners** and **S32**, with existing investors **Addition, Lightspeed, Greylock, GV, and Wells Fargo** participating ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/snorkel-ai-triples-valuation-to-3-5b-as-demand-for-ai-training-data-booms/)). Snorkel shifted from labeling software toward completed datasets and **reinforcement-learning environments**; it cites a **$375M** annualized revenue run rate, an eighteenfold increase over twelve months. TechCrunch’s same piece places Snorkel next to other AI-data labs (Mercor, Handshake, Micro1) whose gross ARR headlines are large but pay-heavy — Snorkel’s pitch is hybrid synthetic + expert data with expert costs in COGS, not in the top-line story. **Lightspeed’s** Sep 1 Healthtech newsletter independently flagged the same structural market: frontier labs hunting healthcare-specific data for pretraining, post-training, and RL ([Lightspeed](https://newsletter.lsvp.com/p/lightspeed-healthtech-summer-newsletter)).

**Network tilt:** founders who sell **finished datasets / RL sandboxes** to labs and regulated enterprises, not another labeling UI; operators who already own proprietary workflow data.

### B. Agent “employees” eating SaaS and services budgets
**Why this week:** Deal narrative moved from copilots to **outcome-priced AI workers**. On Sep 23, **Ema** raised **$77M Series B** led by **Creaegis**, with **Accel, Section 32, and Prosus** increasing — **$140M** total raised; valuation undisclosed but described as more than 4× the 2024 round ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/ema-raises-77m-as-ai-starts-eating-into-enterprise-software-and-services/)). Ema’s product is multi-agent “AI employees” across HR/IT/finance that wrap existing apps, then aim to shrink SaaS dependency. Company-cited traction: **50+** active enterprise deals, **1M+** active enterprise users, **>5M** actions/queries, bookings **>$150M** (multi-year total contract value, not ARR), net dollar retention ~**180%**, gross margins near **80%**, pricing on completed tasks/outcomes. SoftBank Vision Fund’s **Sōzō Insights** landed in the Start Up Markets inbox the same week under the subject *“CFOs' caution: Don't rush to cut jobs”* — the finance-leader framing is **AI-era workforce playbooks**, not slash-and-burn headcount ([visionfund.com / Sōzō](https://visionfund.com/sozo-pulse/cfo-survey-q2-2026)). Together: buyers are reallocating software *and* services dollars; CFOs are cautious on humans-out-first.

**Network tilt:** ex-enterprise platform GTM founders who can sell outcomes into IT/HR/finance; Accel / S32 circles already underwriting the category.

### C. Mega AI funds as structural, not cyclical
**Why this week:** Dry powder at AI-native scale. On Sep 23, **Bessemer Venture Partners** announced **$5.75B** in a single close — **$1.75B** seed/early and **$4B** growth — to back the full AI stack ([Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/news/5-75-billion-to-back-the-founders-building-what-s-next); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/vc-firm-bessemer-now-has-another-5-75b-to-invest-in-what-else-ai/)). Primary post: **260+** AI-native companies backed since 2022 and **>$3B** already invested across compute, models, developer platforms, and agents; ~**70%** of investments still early-stage. Partner **Byron Deeter** (via TechCrunch/Bloomberg): AI-native companies “scaling faster than any category… we’ve backed before,” and companies staying private longer is a “permanent structural shift.” Earlier in the window (Sep 17), **Bain Capital Ventures** closed a **$1.6B** fund (11th; +14% vs prior $1.4B) aimed at “post-AGI” themes: infrastructure, healthcare, physical AI, and security — partner **Kevin Zhang** citing compute until intelligence is “too cheap to meter,” plus portfolio names like Crusoe, Loyal, Dream ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/how-bain-capital-ventures-plans-to-deploy-its-fresh-1-6b-fund/)).

**Network tilt:** Bessemer Atlas / Byron Deeter growth circle; BCV’s paired-partner model for seed–B founders who want PE-adjacent debt and real-economy relationships.

### D. Applied AI product judgment (“can” vs “should”)
**Why this week:** Operator thesis from the First Round inbox. On Sep 23, **First Round Review** published *Parsing “Can” from “Should”: How Netflix’s Product Team Got Thousands of Creatives to Trust an AI Overhaul* — featuring former Netflix GM **Mckenzie Lock** ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/netflix-ai-strategy/)). The punchline: AI strategies fail when built around what models *can* do rather than what humans *should* do; Netflix’s ~300-person distribution org used outcomes-first scenarios, frontline surveys/debates, “today vs tomorrow” storytelling for creatives, and explicit either/or bets (low-stakes first vs tentpoles; measure incrementality vs speed). Memorable operational lesson: an artwork algorithm optimizing viewership would have turned homepages into seas of the same celebrity faces — humans set the multi-metric objective. This is the anti-FOMO complement to Ema/Snorkel capital: **change management is the product**.

**Network tilt:** product leaders who have shipped AI into craft-sensitive orgs; First Round’s Applied Intelligence series as the warm intro surface.

### E. (Honorable) Nature-derived AI drugs into the clinic
On Sep 23, **Enveda** raised **$311M Series E at $2B** (doubled vs ~12 months prior), led by **Catalio**, with **Iconiq** and others — AI discovery from plants/microbes with candidates already in human trials ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/enveda-secures-311m-to-bring-more-nature-derived-ai-drugs-into-clinical-trials/)). Treat as a **capital-markets print** for AI×bio clinical translation, not a new consumer category. Pairs with Lightspeed’s healthtech focus on data/models and clinical AI supply.

## 5. Cooling / crowded

- **Labeling marketplaces as the whole product.** Snorkel’s move to completed datasets + RL environments is the tell: capital wants scarce *finished* training fuel, not another expert-queue UI. If your pitch is “we have humans in a Slack,” you’re late to the Insight/S32 print.
- **AI headcount slash as the slide.** SoftBank Sōzō’s CFO framing in-inbox is caution against rushing job cuts. Buyers (and LPs) want productivity / outcome stories; “fire 30% with GPT” is crowded and politically brittle.
- **“AI strategy” as a can-list.** First Round / Lock: strategies built around model capability without either/or bets and craft buy-in fail at the vision stage. Crowded: vendor demos. Open: judgment systems and measurement of member/business metrics.
- **Foundation-model equity for typical Series A networks.** Still mostly closed — this week’s accessible heat is data suppliers, agent orchestration into existing SaaS, and fund partners with fresh dry powder — not OpenAI/Anthropic primary rounds.
